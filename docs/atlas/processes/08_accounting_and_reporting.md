# 08. Accounting and reporting

*Part of the Philippine PFM Atlas. Facts as of 8 October 2026. The codes in the column "Basis" are explained in [Reference F](../reference/F_sources_and_verification.md). "GK" means general knowledge that I did not open in a source during this work.*

## 1. The question this process answers

Where is each event of budget execution recorded, by which unit and by which body, and how do these records become the reports that DBM, COA, BTr, Congress and the public receive?

The main source of this chapter is the Government Accounting Manual for National Government Agencies of 2015: Volume I for the rules and the entries [GAM-V1], Volume II for the forms [GAM-V2], Volume III for the chart of accounts [GAM-V3]. The present list of budget reports is from the joint circular of 2019 [JC2019-1], which is later than the Manual.

## 2. The idea in plain words

No single record contains a whole transaction. Several units and bodies write about the same money, because each must answer a different question.

Inside one agency there are two main sets of records.

The first question is about legal authority: how much of the appropriation was released, committed and paid? The **budget registries** answer it. A registry is a list with a running balance. It is not a double-entry book. The budget unit keeps it.

The second question is about financial position: what does the agency own and owe, and what did its operations cost? The **accounting books** answer it. They use double entry and the accrual basis. The Manual defines the accrual basis as a basis under which "transactions and other events are recognized when they occur (and not only when cash or its equivalent is received or paid)" [GAM-V1 Ch.2 s.2]. The accounting unit keeps the books.

The Manual states this as the design of the whole system: "The financial reporting system of the Philippine government consists of accounting system on accrual basis and budget reporting system on budget basis" [GAM-V1 Ch.2 s.8].

The two sets do not record the same events. An **obligation** is an event for the registries only. The worked example of the Manual shows no journal entry for the budget law, for the allotment and for the obligation [GAM-V1 Annex H]. A **depreciation** charge is an event for the books only. A **payment** is an event for both.

Outside the agency, three more bodies write about the same money. The servicing bank records what it paid from the account of the agency. BTr keeps the books of the national government for cash and debt. BIR records the tax that the agency withheld. Section 4.3 places all these records side by side.

A case where the two sets of the agency agree: the disbursements in the registry of one period equal the credits to the cash accounts in the books for the same period and the same fund. Both are posted from the same daily reports of checks and payment orders issued [GAM-V1 Ch.3 ss.16-17].

A case where they disagree, without any error: the agency has obligated a contract of 100, has received and accepted deliveries worth 60, and has paid 40. The registry shows obligations of 100 and disbursements of 40. The books show an asset or expense of 60, a payable of 20 and a cash payment of 40. A reader who compares "obligations" with "expenses" finds a difference of 40. This difference is the part that is not yet delivered.

Terms. **RAPAL** is the Registry of Appropriations and Allotments. **RAOD** is the Registry of Allotments, Obligations and Disbursements, kept for each expense class. **RBUD** is the Registry of Budget, Utilization and Disbursements, for funds outside the budget law. **RANCA** is the Registry of Allotments and Notice of Cash Allocation, the record in which the accounting unit follows the cash authority. **JEV** is the Journal Entry Voucher, the form on which one accounting entry is written. **BFARs** are the Budget and Financial Accountability Reports. A **fund cluster** is a group of funds with its own books and reports (chapter 15).

### 4.4 What follows from these tables

**One payment has three dates.** The agency credits its cash account on the day of its daily report, which also contains payment orders "not yet delivered" to the bank [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.52]. The bank credits the payee one or two days after it receives the order. The Treasury records the amount when the bank has paid. A payment order issued on the last day of a month is therefore paid in the books of the agency, and not yet paid in the records of the bank and of the Treasury. None of the three records is wrong.

**The cash account of the agency for its NCA is not money.** The chart of accounts says that the account Cash-MDS, Regular "is used to recognize in the agency books the NCA received for its operating requirements" [GAM-V3 Ch.3]. The Manual says about year-end adjustments of bank accounts that they do not apply to this account, "since there is no actual cash with the GSBs" [GAM-V1 Ch.19 s.56]. GSB means government servicing bank. The account measures a limit that the agency may still use. The money stays in the accounts of the Treasury until the bank has paid (chapter 11).

**Each body has an account that corresponds to an account of another body.** The agency credits Subsidy from National Government, and the Treasury debits Subsidy to NGAs. The agency debits Cash-Treasury/Agency Deposit when it remits a collection, and the Treasury credits the account of the same name. The Manual calls the entry in the books of the other body the "responding entry" [GAM-V1 Annex H]. This is why the Manual requires that the books of each agency are "reconciled with the records of cash transactions maintained by the BTr" [GAM-V1 Ch.2 s.8].

**The two sides do not record at the same moment.** The agency records the subsidy when it receives the NCA, and it reverses the unused part at the end of the period. The Treasury records the subsidy when the bank has paid. At the end of a year the two amounts therefore differ by the checks and payment orders that the agency has issued and the bank has not yet paid. This sentence is my inference from the entries. The Manual does not state it in these words.

**One paper can produce entries in three sets of books without any movement of money.** The Tax Remittance Advice is the example: the agency, BIR and the Treasury each make an entry from it [GAM-V1 Ch.5 s.38].

**The statement that connects both sets.** The Statement of Comparison of Budget and Actual Amounts is one of the six financial statements, and it is prepared from the registries. The Manual gives the reason for a separate statement: "the budget and the financial statements are not prepared on a comparable basis" [GAM-V1 Ch.3 s.25]. The budget unit prepares it. The accounting unit verifies its actual amounts against the Statement of Cash Flows [GAM-V1 Ch.3 s.26, s.30]. Three rules of this statement show how the two sets relate.

- The actual payments in it are the disbursements posted in the registry from the reports of checks and payment orders issued, from the Tax Remittance Advice and from journal entry vouchers [GAM-V1 Ch.3 s.30]. They are cash paid. They are not obligations and not expenses.
- "The NCA received for funding requirements of the agency shall not be considered as receipts for the purpose of this statement" [GAM-V1 Ch.3 s.30]. In the books the NCA is credited to a revenue account, Subsidy from National Government. In this statement it is not a receipt.
- The difference between the final budget and the actual amounts must be reconciled with three balances of the budget report FAR 1: unreleased appropriations, unobligated allotments and unpaid obligations [GAM-V1 Ch.3 s.30]. In symbols of section 12: A minus D equals appropriation not released, plus allotment not obligated, plus obligation not paid.

The notes to the statements must also reconcile the actual amounts of this statement with the amounts of the other statements, and must name three kinds of difference: of basis (the budget is on another basis than the books), of timing (the budget period is not the reporting period) and of entity (the budget omits programs or bodies that the statements include) [GAM-V1 Ch.3 s.28].

## 8. The link between the sets of records

Two things connect a record of one unit with a record of another unit.

The **code** connects records of the same kind of spending. The object code in a line of a registry and the account code in a journal entry are the same code by construction. In the chart of accounts each account has a code of eight digits, and the object code of UACS is this code followed by two digits for the sub-object. Example of the chart: Cash-Collecting Officers has the account code 10101010 and the object code 1010101000 [GAM-V3 Ch.2].

The **document number** connects records of the same transaction. The forms of the Manual carry these numbers.

| Form | Number of another record that it carries | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase order | Number, date and amount of the obligation request, beside the certificate "Funds Available" | GAM-V2 App.61 |
| Inspection and acceptance report | Number of the purchase order, number of the invoice | GAM-V2 App.62 |
| Disbursement voucher | Number of the obligation request. After payment: number of the check or payment order, number of the official receipt of the payee, number of the journal entry voucher | GAM-V2 App.32 |
| Payment order (LDDAP-ADA), each line | Number of the obligation request, and the bank account of the payee | GAM-V2 App.42 |
| Daily report of checks or of payment orders issued, each line | Number of the check or payment order, of the voucher and of the obligation request, with the payee and the object code | GAM-V2 App.13, App.35 |
| Part C of the obligation request | Reference column for the number of the journal entry voucher, check, payment order or Tax Remittance Advice | GAM-V2 App.11 |

The line of the daily report is the one place where the numbers of the payment, of the voucher and of the obligation, the payee and the object code stand together. Chapter 17 follows this chain of numbers from the plan to the asset, and it shows which numbers no form carries.

## 10. Closing a period, and the calendar of one year

At the end of a period the accounting unit makes adjusting entries before it prepares the statements. The Manual lists among them: the unused NCA, the petty cash fund, commercial checks that were written and not released, impairment, the write-down of inventories, corrections, depreciation, and differences of exchange rates [GAM-V1 Ch.19 s.51]. An expense that belongs to the period and is not yet paid is accrued. Example of the Manual: salaries earned and not yet paid are debited to the salary expense and credited to Due to Officers and Employees [GAM-V1 Ch.19 s.52].

At the end of the year the revenue and expense accounts are closed to the account Revenue and Expense Summary, and this account is closed to Accumulated Surplus/(Deficit). The balance of Cash-Treasury/Agency Deposit, Regular is also closed to Accumulated Surplus/(Deficit) [GAM-V1 Ch.19 s.57]. The collections that an agency remitted to the Treasury in the year are therefore not an asset of the agency in the next year.

## 12. Five measures of the same spending

For one program in one year, the budget report, the financial statements, the bank and the performance report show different amounts. Diagram 08c answers the question: which amount measures what, and why do correct records differ?

The idea: one purchase passes five moments on different dates. Authority is given, a commitment is made, a delivery is accepted, cash is paid, and a result is counted. Each moment has its own record and its own report. The totals of a year differ because the moments occur in different periods. The difference is not an error. It is information.

| Measure | Event | First record | Report | What explains the difference to the next measure | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Authority | Appropriation and allotment | Registry of appropriations and allotments | FAR 1 | A minus B is authority not used. It ends when the validity ends | JC2019-1, NBC599, GAM-V1 Ch.3 s.8 |
| B. Obligation | A commitment that will require payment | Obligation request, and the registry of allotments, obligations and disbursements | FAR 1, FAR 1-A | B minus C is committed but not yet delivered or accepted. Part C of the obligation request calls it "Not Yet Due" | JC2019-1, GAM-V1 Ch.3 s.15, GAM-V2 App.11 |
| C. Expense, asset and payable | The delivery is accepted | Journal entry voucher, ledgers | Financial statements to COA | C minus D is accepted but not yet paid. Part C of the obligation request calls it "Due and Demandable". FAR 3 shows the age of these amounts | GAM-V1 Annex H, GAM-V2 App.11, PD1445 s.41, JC2019-1 |
| D. Cash paid | The agency issues the check or payment order, and the bank pays | Daily report of checks and of payment orders issued, then the bank statement | FAR 4, cash operations report of BTr | D can exceed C: cash advances and transfers are paid before any delivery. When a comparison works: two amounts have the same measure, the same period and the same coverage of funds. Example: the obligations in FAR 1 of two agencies for the same year.

When it fails: an obligation rate of 95 percent is read as "95 percent of the work is done". Row B measures commitments. A contract signed in December counts in full, although nothing is delivered. A second example: the bonus rules measure the financial result of an agency as cash disbursements against the NCA received [PBB2024]. That is measure D compared with a cash limit. It says nothing about B, C or E.

### Diagram 08e  One event, several records: what each unit and each body writes for the same purchase (national agency, payment order, tax withheld)

Code file: `../diagrams/mermaid/08e_one_event_several_records.mmd`. Rendered image: [`../rendered/08e_one_event_several_records.png`](../rendered/08e_one_event_several_records.png).

- **Question:** for each event of one purchase, which unit of the agency and which other body writes a record, what does each record say, and where does one body record nothing although another body has recorded the event?
- **Idea:** no single record contains the whole transaction. Five units or bodies write about the same money, each in its own record and on its own date. An obligation exists only in the registries of the budget unit. The first journal entry of the purchase itself is made when the delivery is accepted. The Treasury records the spending of the agency only when the bank has paid. A cell with the words "No record" is therefore information: it shows which body cannot see the event from its own records.
- **How to read it:** each row is one event, in the order of time. Each column is one keeper of records. Column 1 = the event. Column 2 (blue) = budget unit of the agency: registries, no double entry. Column 3 (green) = accounting unit of the agency: books with double entry. Column 4 (white) = cash unit and property unit of the agency. Column 5 (orange) = the servicing bank. Column 6 (yellow) = books of the national government kept by BTr. Column 7 (violet) = books of BIR. Grey with a dashed border = this keeper writes nothing at this event. The diagram has no arrows: the cells of one row are records of the same event, and they are not steps that follow one another.
- **When the records agree:** at the end of a month the cash account of the agency for its NCA equals the balance of the NCA in the bank statement, after the items in transit are listed. The monthly bank reconciliation statement shows this [GAM-V1 Ch.21].
- **When they disagree without an error:** a payment order is issued on the last day of the month. The agency has credited its cash account (row 5). The bank and the Treasury have recorded nothing yet (row 6). The three records differ by this amount until the bank pays.

### Diagram 08c  Five measures of the same spending: the record, the report, and the reason why each differs from the next

Code file: `../diagrams/mermaid/08c_five_measures_of_one_spending.mmd`. Rendered image: [`../rendered/08c_five_measures_of_one_spending.png`](../rendered/08c_five_measures_of_one_spending.png).

- **Question:** for one program in one year, the budget report, the financial statements, the bank and the performance report show different amounts. Which amount measures what, and why do correct records differ?
- **Idea:** one purchase passes five moments on different dates: authority is given, a commitment is made, a delivery is accepted, cash is paid, and a result is counted. Each moment has its own record and its own report. The totals of a year differ because the moments occur in different periods. The difference is not an error. It is information: it shows what is committed but not delivered, delivered but not paid, or paid but not used.
- **How to read it:** each row is one measure, in the order of time. Column 2 = the record in which the event is first written. Column 3 = the report that adds the records. Column 4 (grey) = what explains the difference between this measure and the measure in the next row. Column 5 (violet) = a check that connects two measures.
- **When the comparison works:** two amounts may be compared when they have the same measure, the same period and the same coverage of funds. Example: obligations in FAR 1 of two agencies for the same year.
- **When it fails:** an obligation rate of 95 percent does not show that 95 percent of the goods were delivered or paid. Row B measures commitments, not deliveries or payments.
- **Sources:** registries and the reports FAR 1, FAR 1-A, FAR 1-C, FAR 3, FAR 4 and BAR 1 [JC2019-1]. Which record is written at which event, and the journal entry at the accepted delivery [GAM-V1 Annex H, Ch.3 ss.8-17]. Part C of the obligation request shows obligation, payable and payment of one obligation [GAM-V2 App.11]. The statement of comparison of budget and actual amounts, and the three kinds of difference that it must explain [GAM-V1 Ch.3 ss.25-30]. Bank reconciliation each month [GAM-V1 Ch.21 s.4]. Financial statements and their submission [PD1445 s.41][COA-GAS]. Physical plan (BED 2), cash operations report of BTr, lapse of the NCA, transfers not disbursed until used [NBC599]. The financial result in the bonus rules is cash disbursements measured against the NCA received [PBB2024]. Bank reconciliation: "most active government accounts are reconciled quarterly" (indicator 27.1, score C); unliquidated advances (indicator 27.3, score C) [PEFA2025]. In BTMS the registries, the books and the reports come from one set of records [CL2025-8].
- **A difference between rule and reported practice:** the Manual requires a bank reconciliation statement each month [GAM-V1 Ch.21 s.4]. The assessment of 2025 reports that most active accounts are reconciled each quarter [PEFA2025].

### Diagram 08d  From the reports of each body to national totals: who adds what, and on which basis

Code file: `../diagrams/mermaid/08d_from_agency_reports_to_national_totals.mmd`. Rendered image: [`../rendered/08d_from_agency_reports_to_national_totals.png`](../rendered/08d_from_agency_reports_to_national_totals.png).

- **Question:** no single office holds all the records. Which office adds the figures of which bodies, on which basis of measurement, and into which published product?
- **Idea:** there are several national totals, not one. Each is produced by a different office from a different set of reports, and each uses a different basis: obligations, accrual accounting, or cash. A total of one product cannot be checked against a total of another product without a statement of what differs.
- **How to read it:** left = the bodies that keep records. Middle = the office that collects and adds. Right = the published product, with its basis in capital letters. Dotted arrows are flows of information.
- **The risk of counting twice:** a transfer is spending of the giver and income of the receiver. Example: the National Tax Allotment is spending of the national government and income of the local governments. A total for the whole public sector must remove such transfers. The table of the Department of Finance contains a line for this purpose: "Adjustment in Net Lending and Equity to GOCCs" [BESF2026-A3].
- **The rule for one of the products:** for consolidated financial statements the Manual says "Balances, transactions, revenues, and expenses between entities within the economic entity shall be eliminated in full" [GAM-V1 Ch.20 s.8]. I did not open the Annual Financial Report itself, so I do not know how far it follows this rule. For the cash report of BTr and for the table of DOF I read no rule.
- **Sources:** the duties of each agency, of BTr, of DBM and of COA in the financial reporting system [GAM-V1 Ch.2 s.8]. Budget and financial accountability reports through URS to DBM, COA and BTr [JC2019-1]. Financial statements through the AFR System, and the dates of 14 February and 30 September [PD1445 s.41][COA-GAS]. Statements of government corporations [COA2015-004]. Statements of receipts and expenditures of local governments through eSRE [eSRE]. Cash operations report of BTr [NBC599]. Rows of the Consolidated Public Sector Financial Position, source DOF [BESF2026-A3]. Fiscal Risks Statement of the DBCC and its chapters [FRS2023]. COMPASS portal: budget information from the NEP and the GAA with allotments, obligations and disbursements [COMPASS-LAUNCH][EO126].
- **General knowledge, not opened in a source here:** that local governments send their financial statements to the same sector of COA; that the Annual Financial Report of COA is issued in separate volumes for national agencies, local governments and government corporations; how DOF obtains the data of the social security institutions, the central bank and the government financial institutions.
