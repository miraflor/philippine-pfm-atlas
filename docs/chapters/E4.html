2. The question this process answers

For one payment to a supplier: which officer states which fact, in which document, which record is written at each step, and through which accounts does the money move? And what replaces the contract and the accepted delivery in the other kinds of payment?

The main source of this chapter and of chapter E6 is the Government Accounting Manual for National Government Agencies of 2015: Volume I for the procedures and the entries [GAM-V1], Volume II for the forms [GAM-V2]. Where a later rule changed the Manual, the two chapters give the later rule and say so.

3. The idea in plain words

A payment is the last act of several lines of work that run at the same time. Three things must each be true before money may leave, and each is made true by a different party.

  1. The government is committed, within its authority. The agency holds an allotment and has recorded an obligation against it. The head of the budget unit states this in Box B of the obligation request [GAM-V2 App.11].
  2. The other party has a claim. For a purchase: the goods, services or works were delivered, inspected and accepted. Two officers state this in the acceptance report, each for one fact [GAM-V2 App.62]. The accounting unit then records a payable. The budget reports call such an amount due and demandable [JC2019-1]. Other kinds of payment have another event in this place (chapter E6, section 3.1).
  3. Cash may be used. The agency holds a cash authority (NCA) with enough balance. The head of accounting states this in Box C of the voucher, after a look into the registry of the NCA [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.12][GAM-V2 App.32].

Diagram E4-b shows these three meeting points. A drawing with one line of steps hides them, and this is the main reason why the process is hard to see as a whole.

A record is written at each of these moments. The obligation enters a registry. The accepted delivery produces a journal entry for the payable. The certified voucher is entered in the registry of the NCA. The payment order produces a journal entry for the payment. No record waits for the end of the process. Diagram E4-a names the record at each step.

A case where the process works: the three facts are all true in the same month, and the supplier is paid within days after acceptance.

A case where it fails: the delivery is accepted and the payable is recorded, but the NCA of the quarter has lapsed or is fully used. The head of accounting cannot sign Box C. The Manual says that he or she "notes that cash is not yet available" and that the voucher is kept [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.12]. The claim is valid and stays unpaid. The reverse failure also exists: cash authority is available but the delivery is not accepted before the quarter ends, and the cash authority lapses unused.

A case where two meeting points occur on one day: for many regular expenses, for example a utility bill, the obligation request reaches the budget unit together with the voucher [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.12]. The obligation is then recorded on the day on which the claim is examined. For a purchase with a contract or a purchase order, the obligation is recorded before the order is signed, because the order carries the number of the obligation request [GAM-V2 App.61].

A case where the first meeting point occurs many times for one procurement: under a framework agreement the supplier and the prices are fixed first, and no certificate of funds is needed for the agreement. A certificate is needed for each order under it. The guidelines of the GPPB of July 2026 name such an order a Call-Off [GPPB2026-12]. Chapter E1, section 2.5, and diagram E1-g give the rules.

Terms. The Obligation Request and Status (ORS) is the document that charges an amount to an allotment. The disbursement voucher (DV) is the document that approves one claim for payment. Accounts payable are "valid and legal obligations" of an agency "for which, goods/services/projects have been delivered/rendered/completed and accepted". The LDDAP-ADA (List of Due and Demandable Accounts Payable with Advice to Debit Account) is the payment order to the bank: a list of creditors with the amounts, and below it an instruction to the bank to debit the account of the agency. The Modified Disbursement System (MDS) is the arrangement in which servicing banks pay on agency orders and the Treasury repays the banks. A journal entry voucher (JEV) records one transaction in the accounting books. The registry of the NCA (in the Manual: Registry of Allotments and Notice of Cash Allocation, RANCA) is the list in which the accounting unit compares the allotments received, the NCAs received and the NCAs used [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.2, s.3].

Notes on the steps.

  • Step 3 and the order of the certificate. The Auditing Code requires the certificate of the accounting official before a contract is entered into, and makes a contract without it void [PD1445 ss.86-87]. The form of the purchase order has a place for it, with the number of the obligation request [GAM-V2 App.61]. The step list of the Manual for a construction contract shows another order: the head signs, then the chief accountant certifies, then the budget unit prepares the obligation request for the whole contract price [GAM-V1 Ch.10 s.47].
  • Step 8 and the moment of the payable. The worked example of the Manual records the payable at delivery, from the delivery receipt and the acceptance report [GAM-V1 Annex H]. Its section on payment orders says that payables are recorded "based on approved DVs" [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.57]. The steps follow the worked example.
  • Step 10. The voucher number has four parts: fund cluster, year, month, and a serial number with one series for each fund cluster and year [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.12]. The instructions of the form print a shorter pattern [GAM-V2 App.32]. The number is written on every sheet of the supporting documents.
  • Step 19. The documents are stamped "PAID" before they go to the bank [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.56].
  • Step 22. The Manual says "not later than 48 hours but not earlier than 24 hours" [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.2]. A later circular says within the next two banking days [CL2018-14].
  • Step 27. The daily report contains all approved payment orders of the day, "including those not yet delivered" to the bank [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.52]. The agency also sends a copy of this report to the office that posts it on the website of the agency [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.56].

11. Personnel and payroll

11.1 The question this process answers

Salaries are paid without procurement. What replaces the contract and the delivery, who controls the number of positions and the amount of pay, and to whom does each part of the gross pay go?

11.2 The idea in plain words

In a purchase, three things limit the payment. A contract fixes what is owed, a bid fixes the price, and an acceptance report proves that the supplier performed (chapters E1 and E4). Personnel spending has none of these three. Three other things replace them.

  1. The position replaces the contract. A salary may be paid only for a position that DBM has authorized and that a person holds by a valid appointment.
  2. The salary schedule replaces the bid. The pay of a position follows from its salary grade and step under the compensation rules. The agency and the employee do not negotiate it.
  3. A certificate on the payroll replaces the acceptance report. On the payroll an authorized official certifies: "Services duly rendered as stated" [GAM-V2 App.33]. The attendance record supports this certificate, but it is not always there before the payment. The accounting manual says: "Payrolls are prepared in advance and some agencies pay their employees through the banking system. All these are done before reports of attendance are submitted, making it impossible to know the exact amount to be paid in case there are absences without pay" [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.48]. An overpayment is then demanded back from the employee.

Points 1 and 2 are general knowledge. Point 3 is from the Manual. The sources that I opened also confirm the central record that supports point 1: DBM keeps the list of positions of every agency in the Government Manpower Information System (GMIS), each agency updates its part every month, and DBM computes the personnel budget from the positions that are filled [GMIS].

Two rules of law show how the budget treats personnel in a different way. The budget law does not list the single positions: it "shall not contain any itemization of personal services", and the Secretary of Budget and Management prepares that list for the approval of the President [EO292-B6 s.23, s.21]. And when the President suspends the spending of an agency, the pay of permanent staff is the one item that the law excepts [EO292-B6 s.38].

Because the pay of one position is fixed, the total cost depends almost only on the number of filled positions. The main control of personnel spending is therefore the control of the list of positions, and not the check of each payment. This is my conclusion from the three points.

A case where the process works: a position is in the list, it is filled, and the agency budget of the year already contains its salary. The allotment exists from the first day of the budget law, because personnel services of filled positions are released by the law itself without a release document [NBC599]. The payroll of each period is then a routine payment.

A case where it fails: an agency fills a vacant position in the middle of the year. The agency budget contains no money for it, because the budget was computed from the positions that were filled when the budget was prepared. The money is in a central fund, the Miscellaneous Personnel Benefits Fund, and it is released by a SARO [GMIS][NBC599]. A second process, the request for this release, must therefore proceed at the same time as the payroll (inference from the two sources).

A second case where it fails: the agency does not update its list in GMIS. The personnel budget of the next year is then computed from a wrong number of filled positions (inference from the rule that the budget follows the filled positions).

Terms. A plantilla is the list of the authorized positions of an agency, with the person who holds each one. PSIPOP (Personal Services Itemization and Plantilla of Personnel) is the form of this list that is kept in GMIS. The Miscellaneous Personnel Benefits Fund and the Pension and Gratuity Fund are central funds in the budget law, outside the budgets of the agencies. Retirement and life insurance premiums are the share that the government pays as employer for the insurance of its employees (GK for this description). An Advice for use of PS Allotment (APSA) is the document by which available personnel allotment is used for a shortage in one personnel benefit. A Tax Remittance Advice records tax withheld by an agency as paid to BIR without a movement of cash.

11.5 Where the allotment comes from

Diagram E4-e shows the four sources on its left side. The table gives the same content.

Part of personnel spending Budget item Release document Basis
Salaries and benefits of positions that are filled Personnel services in the budget of the agency None. The budget law itself is the allotment order NBC599 s.3.7, GMIS
New positions, positions filled during the year, and some benefits Miscellaneous Personnel Benefits Fund, a special purpose fund SARO GMIS, NBC599
Government share of retirement and life insurance premiums An automatic appropriation. The law lists it among the items that are appropriated without a yearly vote GARO. The first NCA of the year includes this item NBC599, EO292-B6 s.26

When the allotment for one personnel benefit is not sufficient and allotment for another is available, the head of agency may approve the use by an Advice for use of PS Allotment. For some special benefits the approval of the DBM Secretary is needed [NBC599 s.5.2].

Savings may not pay for new positions or for higher salaries, unless a law specifically allows it [EO292-B6 s.39]. The accounting manual gives each of the three central items its own funding source code: 104102 for retirement and life insurance premiums, 101406 for the Miscellaneous Personnel Benefits Fund and 101407 for the Pension and Gratuity Fund [GAM-V1 Annex P].

Not every person who works for an agency is on this payroll. The procurement rules say that the engagement of a worker under a contract of service or a job order is not procurement [IRR12009 s.4.4]. I did not read the rules under which such workers are engaged and paid.
