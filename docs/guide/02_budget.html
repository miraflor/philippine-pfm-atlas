From a priority to the GAA

An introduction to the atlas. Consult the linked chapters and source notes before operational use.

A spending line begins as a policy proposal, not as money in a bank account. The approved budget results from interacting planning, fiscal and legislative decisions.

Follow the decisions

  1. Plan and appraise. Development priorities and proposed programs or projects enter investment programming and sectoral processes.
  2. Set the fiscal envelope. Macroeconomic, revenue, deficit, expenditure and financing assumptions limit the budget.
  3. Prepare the executive budget. Agencies submit requirements through DBM processes, including the two-tier approach; the President submits the NEP and accompanying documents.
  4. Legislate. Congress considers the proposed budget; the President approves or vetoes items according to applicable law.

Do not infer a strictly linear calendar: planning, fiscal estimates and budget preparation can overlap or be refined iteratively. This sequence describes main decision dependencies.
