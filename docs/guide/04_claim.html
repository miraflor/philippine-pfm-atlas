How a payment becomes due

An introduction to the current procedural atlas; consult linked chapters and source notes before operational use.

Before the government pays, somebody must establish why a particular amount is owed to a particular party—and whether all statutory and documentary conditions are satisfied.

The standard contract-payment path

Procurement planning leads to bidding, evaluation and award where applicable. The certificate of funds, the obligation and the contract follow, then delivery, inspection and acceptance, and the examination of the claim. The Auditing Code requires the certificate before the contract is entered into, and one step list of the Government Accounting Manual shows another order; Reference G, point 60, states both. RA 12009 permits early procurement in defined circumstances but distinguishes that from contract award where funding must be approved and effective.

Not every claim comes from a delivery

The atlas distinguishes payroll, protected advance payments, progress payments, cash aid, transfers, tax refunds and debt service. In particular, an advance to a contractor does not imply that an accepted delivery or ordinary Accounts Payable already exists: its accounting treatment and security requirements must be separately modeled.

For the details, consult Chapter 07 and the special-claims diagram.
