What is recorded, reconciled and audited

An introduction to the atlas. Consult the linked chapters and source notes before operational use.

A single activity can produce a budget registry entry, an accrual accounting entry, a cash record, a property record, and an audit trail at different moments.

Track the event—not just the final payment

The budget unit monitors appropriations, allotments, obligations and disbursements. The accounting unit records transactions under the applicable Government Accounting Manual, such as asset/expense recognition and liabilities after acceptance. Cash and property units maintain their own registers. Periodic reconciliations and financial reports tie those different evidentiary records together.

COA audit and legislative oversight examine the resulting accounts, controls and transactions. Audit is not merely the last box in a workflow: relevant checking and evidence collection occur throughout the year.

Source caution: The atlas records an interpretive issue about the time of Accounts Payable recognition between a worked example and another passage of the Government Accounting Manual. Review Reference G before treating that edge as resolved.
