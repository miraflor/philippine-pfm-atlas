The budget cycle, told in steps

The budget cycle, step by step

Thirty-two steps in four phases: the cycle as the DBM budget-cycle illustration draws it, and as budget practitioners know it.

Scroll down. The same 32 steps then show when each one happens, how one payment clears, and which documents pass from one step to another.

Go to the step explorer · Go to phase summaries

Phase 1 · 12 steps

Preparation

From government priorities to the President's proposed budget. It starts with the Budget Call in January and ends when the President's Budget goes to Congress in July or August, all in the year before the budget year.

For the FY 2027 budget, agencies encoded their Tier 1 and Tier 2 proposals in OSBPS from 6 March to 28 April 2026. [NBM156]

Phase 2 · 5 steps

Legislation

From the President's proposal to an enacted appropriations law: the House, the Senate, the bicameral conference, ratification, and the enactment of the General Appropriations Act.

The FY 2026 GAA, Republic Act No. 12314, took effect on 5 January 2026. [NBC599]

Phase 3 · 6 steps

Execution

From spending authority to contracts, cash allocation and payment. Two of the six steps start before the budget year: early procurement and the budget execution program, from October to December of the prior year.

Phase 4 · 9 steps

Accountability

From performance targets to reporting, review and independent audit. The phase starts before the budget year, with the performance targets, and ends in the year after it, with the DBCC Year-End Report and the audit report.

The same steps, in time

One loop takes about three calendar years

The loop shows the order of the steps. Its timing labels place each step in time: "of the prior FY", "throughout the FY", "within the next FY". On a calendar, the FY 2026 budget runs from the Budget Call in January 2025 to the audit report in 2027.

The months are indicative, not binding deadlines; consult current DBM issuances.

Three budgets in one year

Three budgets are active in one calendar year. In 2026, agencies close the accounts of fiscal year 2025, execute the budget of 2026 and prepare the budget of 2027 (diagram BP-f).Atlas, the big picture, section 2

The faded bars are the same 32 steps for FY 2025 and FY 2027. The moving line is a date in 2026. During much of 2026, steps from three budget years overlap; by October, this illustration shows two.

Inside Execution

Four lines, not one row

The loop draws Execution as one row of six steps. In the atlas, these steps sit on four lines of work that run at the same time: legal authority, the claim, cash, and records and control. Steps from other phases join them: the GAA starts the authority line, and the accountability reports and the audit sit on the records line.

The lines run at the same time. They do not form one line. A payment can be made only at a point where the lines meet. Two lines meet where one act needs a result from both of them. For one payment under a contract there are three such points (diagram BP-d).Atlas, the big picture, section 2

Labels in grey italics are steps that the loop does not draw: the contract, the delivery, the acceptance, the voucher.

One payment under a contract

Meeting point 1

An allotment meets an award. Only then may the agency sign the contract and record the obligation. The procurement law says that no contract may be awarded before the funding is approved and effective [RA12009 s.12]. The Auditing Code says that no contract may be entered into before the accounting official has certified that funds are available [PD1445 s.86].Atlas, the big picture, section 2

One payment under a contract

Meeting point 2

An obligation meets an accepted delivery. Only then is the amount a payable. The Manual defines accounts payable as obligations for which goods, services or projects "have been delivered/rendered/completed and accepted" [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.2]. The budget reports call such an amount due and demandable [JC2019-1].Atlas, the big picture, section 2
The records do not wait for the payment. The allotment and the obligation are entered in registries when they occur, and they have no journal entry. The first journal entry is made when the cash authority arrives. The payable is recorded when the delivery is accepted [GAM-V1 Annex H]. Section 7 and chapter A4 show which unit writes which record, and when.Atlas, the big picture, section 2

One payment under a contract

Meeting point 3

A claim in order meets a cash authority that has enough balance. Only then may the head of accounting certify "Cash available" on the voucher, and only then may the head of the agency approve the payment [GAM-V2 App.32][GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.12]. No check and no payment order may be issued without a covering Notice of Cash Allocation [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.3].Atlas, the big picture, section 2

The same payment, a second ending

When the lines do not meet in time

The delivery is accepted on 28 June, and the voucher reaches the head of accounting on 2 July. The unused part of the cash authority of the second quarter lapsed on the last working day of June [NBC599 s.4.2.1.6]. If the cash authority of the third quarter is needed for other payments, the head of accounting cannot certify that cash is available, and the voucher waits [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.12]. The agency holds a valid obligation and a payable, and it cannot pay. Nothing is wrong in any single line of steps. The lines did not meet in time.Atlas, the big picture, section 2

Back to the loop

Documents connect steps that are far apart

The arrows of the illustration go from each step to the next. Documents also connect steps across the loop. These arrows trace the same budget through its lifecycle; the FY labels elsewhere are a 2026 snapshot, not a single transaction.

  1. 1GAA to allotment release: for items without pre-conditions, the GAA itself is the allotment order. [NBC599]
  2. 2Budget execution program to cash allocation: DBM issues NCAs from the monthly disbursement programs (BED 3). [NBC599] [MC51]
  3. 3Budget execution program to performance targets: the Physical Plan (BED 2) holds the quarterly targets. [NBC599 s.3.9]
  4. 4President's Budget to early procurement: once it is submitted, an agency may start bidding, but may not award. [IRR12009 s.12.1, s.12.4]

Back to the loop

The loop closes through forms and reports

  1. 5Citizen engagement, RDC consultations and program convergence to the agency proposals: BP Forms D, C and 206. [NBM156]
  2. 6Disbursement to accountability reports: obligations and disbursements in the registries are reported in FAR 1, each quarter. [JC2019-1]
  3. 7Accountability reports to performance review: DBM reviews the year from BAR 1 and the financial reports. [PBB2024]
  4. 8Performance review to proposals for the next budget. The atlas marks this use as general knowledge, not a source it read, so the arrow is dashed.

01 / A system of concurrent processes

The four lines and the points where they meet

Public money does not belong to the person who handles it. For this reason a rule decides, at every step, who may do what.

The drawing is wider than the screen. Move it sideways, or read the same lines in the table below it.

Each step links to its chapter. The numbers 1 to 3 mark the meeting points, described below. Section 2 of the big picture explains the lines, and diagram BP-d draws them as a net.

No. Line The question The main steps Who is responsible
1 Legal authority May the government spend for this purpose in this year? Appropriation, then allotment, then obligation Congress, DBM, the budget unit of the agency
2 The claim Why does the government owe this amount to this party? For a purchase: plan, procurement, contract, delivery, acceptance. For a salary: position, appointment, service rendered. Other payments have other steps (section 12) The agency, its Bids and Awards Committee, the supplier or the employee
3 Cash Is there money to pay on the day? Collection and borrowing, the single account of the Treasury, cash authority, payment, replenishment BIR, BOC, the banks, BTr, BSP, DBM

02 / Conditions before payment

Where the lines meet

The lines run at the same time. They do not form one line. A payment can be made only at a point where the lines meet. Two lines meet where one act needs a result from both of them. For one payment under a contract there are three such points (diagram BP-d).

  1. An allotment meets an award. Only then may the agency sign the contract and record the obligation. The procurement law says that no contract may be awarded before the funding is approved and effective [RA12009 s.12]. The Auditing Code says that no contract may be entered into before the accounting official has certified that funds are available [PD1445 s.86].
    Legal authorityThe claim
  2. An obligation meets an accepted delivery. Only then is the amount a payable. The Manual defines accounts payable as obligations for which goods, services or projects "have been delivered/rendered/completed and accepted" [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.2]. The budget reports call such an amount due and demandable [JC2019-1].
    Legal authorityThe claim
  3. A claim in order meets a cash authority that has enough balance. Only then may the head of accounting certify "Cash available" on the voucher, and only then may the head of the agency approve the payment [GAM-V2 App.32][GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.12]. No check and no payment order may be issued without a covering Notice of Cash Allocation [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.3].
    The claimCash

A case where the whole works: an agency starts the bidding before the year begins, which the law allows. The budget law takes effect in January, and for an item without conditions the law itself is the allotment. The agency awards and signs in February, and the budget unit enters the obligation in its registry. The supplier delivers in May, the agency accepts, and the accounting unit records a payable. The Notice of Cash Allocation of the second quarter has enough balance, so the voucher is certified and approved. The agency sends the payment order, the bank credits the supplier, and the Treasury repays the bank [RA12009 s.12][NBC599][CL2013-16A][GAM-V1 Annex H].

A case where it fails: the delivery is accepted on 28 June, and the voucher reaches the head of accounting on 2 July. The unused part of the cash authority of the second quarter lapsed on the last working day of June [NBC599 s.4.2.1.6]. If the cash authority of the third quarter is needed for other payments, the head of accounting cannot certify that cash is available, and the voucher waits [GAM-V1 Ch.6 s.12]. The agency holds a valid obligation and a payable, and it cannot pay. Nothing is wrong in any single line of steps. The lines did not meet in time.

The five parts and their chapters

Part What happens Order in time Chapters
A. Decide Plan, set the fiscal frame, prepare the budget, pass the budget law One after the other, for one fiscal year P0 to P12, L0 to L5
B. Execute Three lines of steps: legal authority, the claim, cash At the same time. Money may leave the Treasury only where the three meet E0 to E6
