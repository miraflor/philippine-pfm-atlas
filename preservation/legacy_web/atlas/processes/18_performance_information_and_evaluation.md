# 18. Performance information and evaluation

*Part of the Philippine PFM Atlas. Facts as of 8 October 2026. The codes in the column "Basis" are explained in [Reference F](../reference/F_sources_and_verification.md). "GK" means general knowledge that I did not open in a source during this work.*

## 1. The question this process answers

The budget law gives each program an amount in pesos and also a target in units, for example a number of classrooms. Who records whether the target was reached, who checks that record, and who uses it? And what is the difference between monitoring, performance review, evaluation and audit?

## 2. The idea in plain words

Money has a line of records: appropriation, allotment, obligation, payment. Performance has a second line of records, parallel to the first.

- The **target** is written in the budget, with the appropriation.
- The **plan** divides the target among the quarters of the year.
- The **quarterly report** states what the agency delivered.
- The **yearly review** by DBM compares the reports with the targets.

The two lines have no arithmetic connection. A report of money says how much was committed or paid. A report of performance says how many units were delivered. The only connection is that both are organized by program. Diagram 08c shows this as measure E beside the four measures of money.

Four different examinations use these records. Each one answers a different question. **Monitoring** asks whether the work is on schedule. **Performance review** asks whether the targets were reached. **Evaluation** asks whether the program caused the result that was intended. **Audit** asks whether the money was spent lawfully and whether the accounts are correct. Diagram 18b shows the four together.

A case where the reported result is reliable: the target is a count of things that the agency itself delivers, for example kilometres of road completed. The count can be checked against contracts and acceptance reports, which are records of the money line.

A case where it is not: the target is a condition that depends on many actors, for example the share of pupils who complete school. The agency can report the figure, but the report does not show whether its spending caused the figure. A program can therefore reach every target and still have no effect, or have an effect that the targets do not show. Only an evaluation can separate the two.

Terms. An **output** is what the agency delivers (a classroom, a training course). An **outcome** is the change that the output is meant to produce (pupils who learn, trained persons who find work). An **indicator** is a measure with a unit, for which a **target** is set for the year. The **program classification** (Program Expenditure Classification, PREXC) is the arrangement of the budget of each agency by programs, each with indicators.

## 3. Authorities and their roles

The law gave this task to the budget department long before the present reports. The Administrative Code of 1987 says: "The President, through the Secretary shall evaluate on a continuing basis the quantitative and qualitative measures of agency performance as reflected in the units of work measurement and other indicators of agency performance, including the standard and actual costs per unit of work" [EO292-B6 s.51]. The same chapter requires standard costs for each unit of work, a continuing review of the programs of each agency, and a report of each head of agency every six months on "both work and financial results" [EO292-B6 s.54, s.55, s.56]. A failure to submit the required reports stops the salary of the officials responsible [EO292-B6 s.57]. The present reports BED 2 and BAR 1 are the form in which these duties are performed today. This last sentence is my reading, and the circulars that I read do not cite these sections.

## 5. Four uses of the reported results

### 5.1 The bonus of the personnel

A Performance-Based Bonus pays the personnel of an agency according to the results of the agency. The rules for the bonus of FY 2024 name four criteria of 25 points each [PBB2024].

| Criterion | What is measured | Record used | Who validates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Performance results | Achievement of the targets that Congress approved under the program classification in the budget law | BAR 1 | DBM |
| Process results | Improvement of one service to the public, compared with the year before | A form of the agency in an online portal | Anti-Red Tape Authority |
| Financial results | Disbursement budget utilization rate: cash disbursements measured against the NCA received | FAR 4 and the NCA records | DBM |
| Citizen and client satisfaction | Resolution of complaints received through the hotline 8888 and the Contact Center ng Bayan | Records of the two services | Offices that operate the two services |

An agency needs at least 70 points. The highest bonus is 65 percent of the monthly basic salary [PBB2024].

Two facts limit the use of the bonus as a control. First, the system is under review. Executive Order No. 61 of 3 June 2024 suspended the earlier performance management system and the incentive system, because they were "duplicative and redundant with the internal and external performance audit and evaluation systems", and created a working group to design a simpler system [EO61]. Second, the decision is late. The rules for the bonus of FY 2024 were issued on 3 July 2026, 18 months after the end of that year [PBB2024].

Note the financial criterion. It measures cash paid against cash authority received, which is measure D of diagram 08c. An agency can score well on it by paying quickly, whether or not the outputs were delivered.

### 5.2 The public portal

The COMPASS portal of DBM, launched on 26 June 2026, shows budget information by department, agency, region and purpose [COMPASS-LAUNCH]. Executive Order No. 126 of 28 September 2026 requires national agencies and government corporations to publish through it the financial performance and the implementation status of programs and projects. The order makes each agency responsible "for the accuracy, completeness, and timeliness of the information it submits" [EO126]. The portal therefore shows what agencies report. The documents that I read describe no check of this information by another body before publication. Chapter 16, section 11, gives what is known about the portal and the order.

### 5.3 Evaluation

The National Evaluation Policy Framework was issued by the planning authority and DBM in 2015 and revised on 14 April 2025. It "calls for the purposive conduct of independent evaluation of government programs and projects", assessed "in terms of their efficiency, outcomes and impacts based on national priorities" [NEPF]. The revision introduced a national evaluation agenda and evaluation agendas of agencies, aligned with the development plan, and a steering committee [NEPF-NEWS]. I did not open the text of the revised circular. How the results of an evaluation must be used in the budget process is not stated in the sources I read.

### 5.4 Audit and Congress

COA audits each agency after payment (chapter 09). The 2025 assessment rates the examination of audit reports by the legislature D: no formal examination is made, only hearings when a need arises [PEFA2025].

## 9. Controls: what each one checks, and what it does not check

| Control | What it checks | What it does not check |
|---|---|---|
| BAR 1 against BED 2 | That reported outputs are compared with planned outputs each quarter | That the reported outputs exist. The agency reports on itself |
| Validation by DBM for the bonus | The reported achievement of the targets | Whether the targets were demanding, and whether the outputs had an effect |
| Financial criterion of the bonus | That cash authority was used | That the payments were for accepted deliveries. Advances and transfers also count as payments |
| Evaluation | Whether the program caused the intended result | All programs. Only chosen programs are evaluated |
| Audit | Legality of payments, correctness of accounts | Whether the program was the best use of the money |
| Publication in COMPASS | That the public can see what agencies report | The accuracy of the reports. The order leaves this responsibility with each agency |

Basis of the rows: JC2019-1, PBB2024, NEPF, COA2011-002, EO126. The second column of each row is my reading of what the source describes.

- **Question:** the budget law contains targets in units (for example classrooms built), not only amounts in pesos. Where is a target written, who reports the result, who checks the report, and who uses it?

### Diagram 18b  Four ways to examine performance, and the question that each one answers

Code file: `../diagrams/mermaid/18b_four_examinations_of_performance.mmd`. Rendered image: [`../rendered/18b_four_examinations_of_performance.png`](../rendered/18b_four_examinations_of_performance.png).

- **Question:** monitoring, performance review, evaluation and audit are often named together. What is the difference between them: who does each one, when, on which records, and what may follow from it?
- **Idea:** the four examinations answer four different questions about the same program. A program can pass one and fail another. Example: a training program reports all planned courses as held (monitoring and review: target reached), while an evaluation finds that the trained persons did not find work (the intended effect did not occur), and an audit finds that all payments were lawful.
- **How to read it:** each row is one kind of examination. Column 2 = who does it and when. Column 3 = the question. Column 4 = the records used. Column 5 (grey) = what may follow.
- **Sources:** row 1, quarterly reports BAR 1 and FAR 1 [JC2019-1]; monitoring of physical and financial progress by the implementing agencies (indicator 11.4, score B) [PEFA2025]; monitoring of selected projects with digital images [ROADMAP-MID]. Row 2, Agency Performance Review and the bonus criteria [PBB2024]; review of the earlier systems, which the order calls "duplicative and redundant with the internal and external performance audit and evaluation systems" [EO61]. Row 3, independent evaluation of programs and projects "in terms of their efficiency, outcomes and impacts", national and agency evaluation agendas, steering committee [NEPF][NEPF-NEWS]. Row 4, audit after payment and the notices that may follow [COA2011-002][COA2009-006]; no formal examination of audit reports by the legislature (indicator 31, score D) [PEFA2025].
- **General knowledge, not opened in a source here:** the performance audit of COA as a separate kind of audit, and the consequences in column 5 of rows 1 to 3.
