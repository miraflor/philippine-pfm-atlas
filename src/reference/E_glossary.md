# Reference E. Glossary and abbreviations

*Part of the Philippine PFM Atlas. Each definition is in plain words. The chapter where the term is explained in full is given in brackets. The third column names a term or an idea that is similar to the term and means something different. An empty cell means that I have nothing to add there.*

## 1. Terms

| Term | Meaning | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts payable | Amounts that the government owes for deliveries already accepted. The Manual calls them valid and legal obligations for which goods, services or projects were delivered, rendered or completed, and accepted. (E4) | An obligation, which exists from the contract on, before any delivery |
| Accrual basis | A way of accounting that records an expense when the goods or services are received, not when the cash is paid. The Government Accounting Manual uses it. (A4) | The budget reports, which count obligations and disbursements. The two give different amounts for the same year |
| Advance payment | A payment under a contract before the delivery. The law allows it only with the approval of the President. The procurement rules of 2025 name the cases, and in several of them they require a security, for example a bank guarantee. The advance is recovered from later payments. (E6) | A cash advance, which is given to an officer of the agency and not to a supplier |
| Allotment | An authorization by DBM that allows an agency to incur obligations up to an amount, for a purpose and a period. It contains no cash. (E0) | The appropriation, which is the law, and the Notice of Cash Allocation, which is the limit for payments |
| Allotment class, also expense class | One of four groups of spending: personnel services, maintenance and other operating expenses, financial expenses, capital outlays. (E0) | The object code of UACS, which is a finer class inside an allotment class |
| Appropriation | An authorization made by law to pay money from the Treasury for a stated purpose. (L0) | Money in an account. An appropriation is a permission |
| Appropriation ordinance | The budget law of a local government, enacted by its sanggunian. (E3) | The General Appropriations Act, which is national |
| Augmentation | Adding to an item of appropriation that is deficient, from savings in other items. Only five officials named in the Constitution may be authorized to do it, each for the own office. (E3) | A modification, which changes amounts inside one item and needs no savings |
| Automatic appropriation | An appropriation made by a standing law, so that Congress does not vote the amount each year. Examples: interest payments, the National Tax Allotment. (L5) | An automatic release. Several automatic appropriations still need a release document of DBM |
| Availability payment | A regular payment by the government to the private partner of a partnership for keeping a facility available, whether or not the public uses it. (P1) | A government undertaking. The Code treats the two as different things |
| Bicameral conference | A meeting of members of the House and the Senate that reconciles two versions of a bill. (L0) |  |
| Books of accounts | The journals and ledgers that the accounting unit keeps by double entry, for each fund cluster. (A4) | The registries of the budget unit. Both record the same spending, at different moments |
| Budget Accountability Report (BAR) | A report on physical or other performance against targets; BAR No. 1 is the Quarterly Physical Report of Operations. It must not be read as proof of expenditure or independently verified output. (A4, section 6) | A Financial Accountability Report (FAR), which deals with amounts and balances. |
| Budget and Financial Accountability Reports (BFARs) | The prescribed family of physical and financial accountability returns, including BAR and FAR forms. Different reports measure different dimensions of a budget and should not be mistaken for proof that a transaction was lawful or that a service was delivered. (A4, section 6; Reference C) | One financial report; BFAR refers to the family, not to a single form. |
| Budget Call | The yearly memorandum by which DBM tells agencies how to prepare their proposals. (P0) | The budget ceiling of an agency, which is one content of the Call |
| Budget Execution Document (BED) | A prescribed agency planning document for financial, physical or monthly disbursement programming. A BED is a plan, not a report of actual spending. (E2) | A Financial Accountability Report, which records execution after the event. |
| Call-Off | An order under a framework agreement. It is a purchase order, and a certificate of availability of funds is issued for each one. (E1) | The framework agreement itself, for which no certificate is issued |
| Capital outlays | Spending on assets that last more than one year, such as buildings, roads and equipment. (E0) | Infrastructure only. Capital outlays also include equipment |
| Capitalization threshold | The unit cost from which an item is recorded as property, plant and equipment: PHP 50,000 since 2022. The Manual of 2015 says PHP 15,000. (A4) | The limit for one payment from petty cash, which is another rule |
| Carrier | In the register of interfaces: the means by which a flow passes from one body or system to another, for example paper, entry by the sender, or system to system. (IS) | The kind of the flow, which says what passes: data, a document, an authority or money |
| Cash advance | Money given to a named officer before the expense is made. The officer must later account for it. The Manual names four kinds: for payroll, for operating expenses of a field office, for travel, and for a special purpose. (E6) | An advance payment to a contractor under a contract |
| Cash budgeting | The rule that an appropriation may be obligated and paid only within its fiscal year, with a short extended payment period. In force since 2019. (E0) | Accounting on the cash basis. The books of agencies use the accrual basis |
| Cash program | The plan of cash receipts and payments of the government by month. (E5) | The Notice of Cash Allocation, which is the limit for one agency that follows from the plan |
| Certificate of availability of funds | The statement of the chief accountant, before a contract is entered into, that funds are available for it. A contract without it is void. (E0, E4) | Box B of the obligation request, where the budget unit certifies the allotment, and Box C of the voucher, where the accountant certifies cash |
| Claim | In this atlas: the event by which the government owes an amount to a named party. A purchase that was delivered and accepted is one form. A salary for service rendered, a transfer and debt service are other forms. (E4) | An obligation, which is the commitment in the registry, and a voucher, which is the document that approves the claim for payment |
| Classification | A system of codes that puts amounts into classes. UACS is a classification. A classification code does not identify one transaction. (UACS) | An identifier |
| Commitment | In BTMS, an amount reserved against an allotment before the obligation is recorded. (IS) | An obligation. In BTMS the commitment comes first |
| Common fund | The rule that a regular NCA may pay obligations of the current year and unpaid obligations of earlier years. (E0) | The General Fund |
| Competitive bidding | The general mode of procurement, open to all eligible bidders. (E1) |  |
| Consolidation | Adding the statements of several entities into one statement, after the balances and transactions between them are removed. (A6) | Reconciliation, which compares two records of the same thing |
| Constructive receipt | A receipt that is recorded although no cash moves. Example: tax that an agency withholds is recorded as remitted to BIR by a Tax Remittance Advice. (E5, A4) | A deposit of cash |
| Contingent liability | A promise of the government to pay if a stated event occurs, for example a guarantee of a loan. (P1) | Debt and accounts payable, which are owed without a condition |
| Disallowance | A decision of the auditor that an expenditure was illegal, irregular, unnecessary, excessive or extravagant. The persons named must return the amount. (A9) | A suspension, which only holds an amount until a requirement is met |
| Disbursement | A payment. In budget reports, the settlement of an obligation. (E6) | An expense in the books, which is recorded at acceptance, and a release of allotment by DBM |
| Due and demandable | A claim is due and demandable when the goods, services or works were delivered, rendered or completed, and accepted. (E4) | Obligated. An obligation becomes due and demandable only after acceptance |
| Early procurement | Starting a procurement before the budget law is in force. No award may be made until the funding is approved and effective. (E1) | An award before the funding exists, which the law does not allow |
| Electronic Marketplace | A facility of PhilGEPS in which agencies order common-use supplies and equipment from merchants that the Procurement Service has qualified. The agency pays through an eWallet, a Government Purchase Card or another electronic mode, and the Procurement Service pays the merchant after the acceptance. (E1) | The Virtual Store, the other facility of PhilGEPS for common-use items |
| Enrolled bill | The final text of a bill as passed by both chambers and sent to the President. (L0) | The law. The bill is not yet law when it is sent to the President |
| Evaluation | An examination of whether a program caused the result that was intended, at a reasonable cost. (A3) | Monitoring (is the work on schedule), performance review (were the targets reached) and audit (was the money spent lawfully) |
| Expense | In the books of an agency: the cost of goods or services received in a period, whether or not they are paid. (A4) | An obligation, which is a commitment, and a disbursement, which is cash paid |
| Financial Accountability Report (FAR) | A numbered financial return prescribed for agencies, with distinct forms for appropriations and allotments, obligations, disbursements, off-budget funds, revenue, and trust receipts. See the FAR No. 1â€“6 list in A4, section 6, and Reference C. | BAR 1, a physical-output report, and an audited financial statement. |
| Financial statements | Reports derived from accrual accounting records, such as financial position, financial performance and cash flows. They are not interchangeable with the budgetary execution measures in FAR 1. (A4) | Financial Accountability Reports, which use budget-execution reporting bases. |
| Fiscal risk | An event that would make revenue, spending or debt differ from the plan. (P1) |  |
| Fiscal space | The spending program minus the total cost of ongoing programs (Tier 1). It is the amount available for new or expanded programs. (P0) | Savings |
| Fiscal year | The budget year. In the Philippines it is the calendar year. |  |
| Forward Estimates | The estimated cost of ongoing programs for the next three years. (P0) | The Forward Obligational Authority, a document for projects financed by foreign loans |
| Framework agreement | An option contract for one, two or three years that fixes the terms and the prices of later orders with one supplier or with several. The agency may order or not order. (E1) | A contract for more than one year, which binds the government for every year and needs a Multi-Year Contractual Authority |
| Fund cluster | A group of funds that must be recorded and reported separately. There are seven. The cluster is the first two digits of the funding source code. (E6) | A bank account. A cluster is a group in the records. Its cash may be a limit in a sub-account at a servicing bank, or an account in a depository bank |
| General Fund | The fund that receives all government money for which no law provides a separate fund. (E5) | The Treasury Single Account, which is a structure of bank accounts. The General Fund is a category in the books |
| Government Accounting Manual | The Commission on Audit manual for national government agencies: Volume I addresses policies and procedures, Volume II accounting books, registries and forms, and Volume III the Revised Chart of Accounts. The original edition is from 2015 and subsequent circulars may modify its provisions. (A4, Reference F) | A DBM budget execution circular or a current service requirement. |
| Government undertaking | Any contribution or support that the government gives to the private partner of a partnership, for example a subsidy or a guarantee. (P1) | An availability payment |
| Hash | A short number computed from a file. Any change in the file changes the number. (IS) | The file itself. The file cannot be rebuilt from the number |
| Head of the procuring entity | The official who approves procurement plans and awards. For an agency this is the head of agency or an authorized official. (E1) | The Bids and Awards Committee, which recommends. The head decides |
| Identifier | A number that names one record only, for example one voucher. Different from a classification code. (UACS) | A classification code |
| Indicator | A measure with a unit, for which a target is set for the year. (A1) |  |
| Inspection and acceptance | Two acts by two officers. The inspection officer or committee states that quantity and specifications are in order. The property custodian accepts the delivery. The payable is recorded after them. (E4, A4) | Delivery alone. Goods that are delivered and not yet accepted create no payable |
| Interface | A defined way in which one program sends data to another program and receives data from it, without a person who enters the data again. In the register of interfaces this is the carrier "System to system". (IS) | A report or a file that a person sends, or data that a person types a second time. Reference B, section 4, separates these cases |
| Item veto | The power of the President to reject particular items of an appropriation bill and approve the rest. (L0) | A veto of the whole bill |
| Journal entry | A record in the accounting books with equal debit and credit amounts. It is written on a journal entry voucher. (A4) | An entry in a registry of the budget unit. Appropriations, allotments and obligations are entered in the registries and not in the journal |
| Kind of flow | In the register of interfaces: what passes. One of four: data, a document, an authority, money. (IS) | The carrier |
| Liquidated damages | An amount that a supplier or contractor owes for each day of delay, deducted from the payment. (E1, E6) | Liquidation |
| Liquidation | The report, with documents, by which an officer or a receiving body shows how advanced or transferred money was spent. (E6) | Liquidated damages, which are a penalty for late delivery under a contract |
| Modification | A change inside an allotment, for example between objects of expenditure. (E3) | Augmentation |
| Monitoring | The regular check by the agency itself whether an activity is on schedule and inside its budget. (A1) | Evaluation and audit |
| Monthly Disbursement Program | The plan of payments of an agency by month. It is the basis of the NCA. (E2, E5) | The NCA itself |
| Multi-Year Contractual Authority | A permission of DBM to enter into a contract that lasts more than one year and is paid from local funds. (E1) | An appropriation. The money of each year still comes from the budget law of that year |
| National Tax Allotment | The share of local governments in national taxes: 40 percent of the collections of the third fiscal year before the budget year. (E3) | The Notice of Transfer of Allocation, which other documents shorten to the same three letters |
| Net lending | The amount that the national government lends to government corporations, less repayments. (P1) | Subsidy and equity to the same corporations, which are not loans |
| Notice of Cash Allocation | The limit up to which the servicing bank of an agency may pay from the sub-account of the agency in a period. (E0, E5) | Cash in an account of the agency. No money moves when the notice is issued. The bank pays first, and the Treasury repays the bank |
| Obligation | A commitment by the government that will lead to a payment, for example a signed contract or a payroll. It is charged to an allotment. The Administrative Code defines it as an amount committed to be paid for a lawful act of an authorized officer. (E4) | A delivery, an expense or a payment. An obligation is recorded before all three |
| Off-budget funds | Money that an agency receives and uses under a special law without a yearly appropriation: retained income, revolving funds, trust receipts. (E5, E6) | Unprogrammed appropriations, which are in the budget law |
| Operating unit | An entity that receives release documents directly from DBM. (E0) | A department. One department can have several operating units |
| Outcome | The change that an output is meant to produce, for example pupils who learn. (A1) | An output |
| Output | What an agency delivers, for example a classroom or a training course. (A1) | An outcome, and the money spent. A high rate of obligations is not an output |
| Performance review | The yearly comparison by DBM of the reported results of an agency with the targets in the budget law. (A5) | Evaluation and audit |
| Performance security | A guarantee that the winning bidder posts before the contract is signed. It is released after final acceptance. (E1) | Retention money, which the agency keeps from payments, and the bid security, which covers the time before the award |
| Personnel services | Spending on salaries, wages and other compensation. Also written "personal services". (E4) |  |
| Petty cash fund | A small amount of cash kept by a custodian for expenses that cannot conveniently be paid by check or payment order. (E6) | A cash advance for one purpose, which ends when the purpose is served |
| Plantilla | The approved list of positions of an agency. (E4) | The payroll, which lists the persons who are paid in one period |
| Post-qualification | The check of the documents and capacity of the bidder with the best bid, before award. (E1) | The first check of eligibility documents, which comes before the evaluation of the bids |
| Pre-audit | Examination of a transaction by the auditor before payment. COA lifted it in 2011 and may restore it where internal control is weak. (A9) | The checks by the accounting unit of the agency before payment, which still exist |
| Program, activity, project | The three kinds of budget line. A program groups activities and projects that serve one outcome. (UACS) |  |
| Progress payment | A payment for the part of a works contract that is done and certified. The contractor may bill once a month. (E6) | An advance payment, which is made before any work |
| Public-private partnership | A contract by which a private firm finances, builds or operates a public facility and is paid later by the government or by the users. (P1) | A purchase under the procurement law. A partnership follows its own Code |
| Quick Response Fund | An amount kept ready inside the budget of an agency, or inside the disaster fund of a local government, that may be used immediately after a disaster. (E6) | The national disaster fund, whose use needs the approval of the President |
| Re-enacted budget | The budget law of the previous year, which stays in force when no new law has been passed. (L0) |  |
| Reconciliation | The comparison of two records of the same thing, with a list of the items that explain the difference. Example: the books of an agency and the bank statement. (A4) | Consolidation. A reconciliation does not add records. It explains why two records differ |
| Recoupment | The recovery of an advance payment by a deduction from each later payment. (E6) | Retention money |
| Refund of a tax | The return of tax that was collected and is not due. It needs an appropriation, and it reduces net revenue. (E5) |  |
| Registry | A running list with balances and without double entry. The budget unit keeps the registries of appropriations, allotments, obligations and disbursements. The accounting unit keeps the registry of allotments and cash authority. (A4) | The books of accounts, which are journals and ledgers with double entry |
| Responsibility center | A part of an agency whose head is responsible for a set of activities. Its code is the organization code of UACS with three more digits. (UACS) | An operating unit, which receives release documents from DBM |
| Retention money | A part of each progress payment that the agency keeps until the work is finally accepted. It covers defects. (E6) | A performance security, which the contractor posts |
| Reversion | The return of an unused amount to the General Fund when the validity of the appropriation ends. (E0) | The lapse of an NCA at the end of a quarter. A lapsed NCA ends a cash limit. It does not end the allotment |
| SAAODB | Statement of Appropriations, Allotments, Obligations, Disbursements and Balances (FAR No. 1). A quarterly summary of the stages and remaining balances of agency budget execution. (A4, section 6; Reference C) | A financial statement on the accrual basis, or evidence that physical outputs were delivered. |
| Sanggunian | The legislature of a local government. (E3) |  |
| Savings | Parts of an appropriation that are not needed after the purpose was completed or abandoned. The exact definition is in each budget law. (E3) | Any unused balance. An amount that was only not spent is not savings in this sense |
| Schedule of Market Values | The list of values of land and buildings in a local government, prepared by its assessor, from which the real property tax is computed. (E3) | The tax rate, which the sanggunian sets |
| Semi-expendable property | A tangible item below the capitalization threshold. It is recorded as inventory when received and as an expense when issued, and it is still issued to a named officer. (A4) | Property, plant and equipment, which stays in the books as an asset |
| Special account in the General Fund | Money that a law earmarks for a purpose. It is in the Treasury but may be used only for that purpose. (E5) | A separate bank account of the agency |
| Special purpose fund | A lump-sum appropriation for a purpose, not assigned to one agency at the start, for example the Contingent Fund. (L5) | A special account in the General Fund, and a fund cluster |
| Stage of use | In the register of interfaces: whether a flow is in use, in use for part of the users, described or required without proof of use, or planned. (IS) | The carrier. A flow can be in use on paper |
| Subsidy from National Government | The account that an agency credits when it receives a Notice of Cash Allocation. The unused part is reversed at the end of the period. (A4) | A subsidy to a government corporation, which is a payment |
| Suspension | A decision of the auditor that holds an amount in question until a missing requirement is met. (A9) | A disallowance |
| Tier 1 and Tier 2 | The two parts of a budget proposal: the cost of ongoing programs, and new or expanded programs. (P0) |  |
| Treasury Single Account | A unified structure of government bank accounts that lets BTr see and use the cash of the government as one balance. (E5) | One bank account, and the General Fund |
| Trial balance | A list of all accounts of the general ledger with their balances at one date. (A4) | A financial statement. The statements are prepared from it |
| Trust receipts | Money that the government holds for a specific purpose or for another party. (E5) |  |
| Unified Accounts Code Structure (UACS) | The common classification for government budget and accounting information, including organizational, funding, program and object segments. It classifies an amount but does not uniquely identify a transaction. (UACS) | Document or transaction identifiers, such as an individual ORS or DV number. |
| Unified Reporting System (URS) | The DBM platform through which agencies submit prescribed budget execution and financial accountability returns under applicable rules. (IS, A4) | BTMS, a broader budget-execution and accounting system with its own rollout status. |
| Unprogrammed appropriations | Standby appropriations in the budget law that may be released only when stated conditions are met. (L5) | Off-budget funds, which are not in the budget law |
| Validity | The period in which an appropriation may be obligated and paid. (E0) | The period of an NCA, which is shorter |
| Variation order | A change of a works contract after the award. For goods the name is amendment to order. The rules limit the increase of the price. (E1) | A new contract |
| Viability gap funding | A subsidy that makes a partnership project financially possible for the private partner when the income from users is too low. (P1) |  |
